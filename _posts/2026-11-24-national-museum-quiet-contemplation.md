Our Short moves from the **Room of Quiet Contemplation** to the outdoor pond at the **National Museum of Korea** in Yongsan. The visit works well when you want a slower indoor stop, rather than another shopping queue.

## What makes the room worth slowing down for?
The museum identifies the two gilt-bronze pensive bodhisattvas as National Treasures, made in the late sixth and early seventh centuries. In a dim gallery, their contemplative poses invite a closer look at expression and shape. Keep conversation low: the museum asks all visitors, including guided groups, to maintain silence.

## What is free?
The permanent galleries are free. Some special exhibitions charge separately, so check the exhibition page before assuming a particular show is included. The room is one part of the museum, rather than a separate attraction requiring its own paid tour.

## Build a simple visit
Begin with the room, leave time for another gallery that interests you, and finish outside by the pond if the weather is comfortable. The address is **137 Seobinggo-ro, Yongsan-gu, Seoul**. Our video description suggests approaching from Ichon Station, Exit 2; allow time for the walk and follow the museum signs.
