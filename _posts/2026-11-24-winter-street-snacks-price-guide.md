Our newer winter street-food Short returns to **bungeoppang, hotteok and gyeranppang**, this time with more detail on price differences and how to order. It is a separate upload from our earlier [winter-snack introduction](/stories/korean-winter-street-snacks/).

## Compare the portion, not just the number
| Snack | What it is | Indicative prices quoted in the video description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bungeoppang (붕어빵) | Fish-shaped bread with a filling | Three for ₩2,000 at some stalls; individual prices can be higher |
| Hotteok (호떡) | Fried dough pancake, often with sweet filling | Around ₩1,500–2,000 locally; more in some visitor areas |
| Gyeranppang (계란빵) | Small bread baked with an egg | Around ₩1,500–2,000 locally; some visitor-area stalls charge more |

These figures come from the November 2026 video description and are examples, not citywide fixed prices. Ask whether the displayed price is for one piece or a bundle. Filled, larger and specialty versions may cost more.

## Three useful ordering details
For bungeoppang, **pat (팥)** means red bean and **syukeurim (슈크림)** means custard-style filling. Pointing at the Korean label is useful when a stall sells several varieties.

For hotteok, let the filling cool before taking a large bite. Sweet fillings can be much hotter than the outside suggests. Check the stall's ingredient information if you need to avoid nuts or other allergens.

For gyeranppang, expect egg as a defining ingredient. Ask about other ingredients instead of assuming that a small street snack is suitable for your diet.

## Plan a flexible snack stop
Stall locations, operating days and seasonal hours vary. Bring a payment option the vendor accepts and confirm the price before ordering; do not make a special journey based only on an old bargain-price post. One hot snack is an easy add-on to a neighbourhood walk, while three in a row is better shared.
