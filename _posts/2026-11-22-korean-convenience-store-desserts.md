Our convenience-store Short is less about a long shopping list and more about three different textures: chewy with a crisp filling, a chocolate shell with a cold centre, and a biscuit sandwich around chocolate. Here is how to use those ideas on your next snack run.

## Du-jjon-ku: chewy meets crunchy
The Short describes du-jjon-ku as a Korean take on the Dubai-chocolate trend, with kataifi and pistachio cream. Its appeal is the contrast between a chewy bite and the crisp strands inside.

Names and spellings on packages may vary. Match the product and its filling rather than assuming every pistachio dessert is the same item shown in the video. Check the ingredient label if nuts, dairy or wheat matter to you.

## Wak-ppu: the shell is part of the experience
The second dessert is introduced through the action of cracking a chocolate shell to reach the cold cream inside. If that contrast is what you want, check where the product is displayed and follow its storage instructions.

Plan to eat a chilled or frozen purchase soon after leaving the store unless you can keep it at the required temperature. A long walk with it in a warm bag will change the experience.

## Bichobi: chocolate between crisp biscuits
Bichobi is the biscuit-and-chocolate option in the video. It makes a useful contrast if you are comparing textures rather than buying several similar cream desserts. Check the package size before deciding how much to buy for a group.

## Make a small tasting stop
The Short suggests a CU or GS25 run, but stock differs by store and the featured items are not guaranteed to be available at both chains. Use the video as inspiration, then check the shelf, label and price in front of you.

If you are sharing, buy a small selection and compare the shell, filling and biscuit separately. There is no need to buy every variation to find the texture you like.
