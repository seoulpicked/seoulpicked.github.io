Our winter street-food Short puts three snacks side by side: **bungeoppang (붕어빵)**, **hotteok (호떡)** and **egg bread, or gyeran-ppang (계란빵)**. Use the video as a starting point for your next snack stop, then check what is actually available at the stall.

## Start with the names
Bungeoppang is the fish-shaped bread featured in the video; red-bean and custard fillings are mentioned in its description. If you have a preference, ask about the filling before ordering rather than relying on the shape alone.

Hotteok and egg bread are the other two choices in the Short. Pointing to the displayed snack or its name on the menu is a straightforward way to order if you are not confident pronouncing it.

## Read the price as a quantity
The video includes price examples from its upload period. They are not a fixed price list for every stall. Check whether the sign quotes one piece or a bundle, confirm the filling, and ask for the total before paying. Stock and payment options can also vary by seller.

## Keep the order simple
For a first stop, choose one snack that interests you instead of buying everything at once. A useful phrase is **“One, please”**, with a gesture toward the item. Ask the seller about ingredients if you have a food allergy or need to avoid something; a short video cannot establish what a particular stall uses.
