The rice cake in our Short is built around a simple visual surprise: cut through the chewy outer layer and a piece of fruit is waiting inside. Hanjungsun—also written Hanjeongseon in some English guides—is the brand featured in this Seoul dessert stop.

## Start with the filling
The video description mentions whole-fruit options such as strawberry, tangerine and kiwi, plus a Greek-yogurt line. It also highlights chocolate-and-pistachio combinations inspired by Dubai-style desserts and a red-bean-and-butter option.

Choose the flavour you want to eat, rather than buying an entire box for the cross-section photo. Whole fruit and cream make these different from a dry souvenir snack: think about when you will eat them and how you will carry them.

## Plan for takeaway
Our Short identifies the Seongsu flagship at **43-1 Yeonmujang-gil** and describes takeaway service and on-site queue registration. Check the current branch arrangement when you arrive. A queue shown in a video is a snapshot; a two-hour wait is not a guaranteed or typical wait for every day.

If you have a fixed train time or another reservation, set a limit on how long you can wait. Ask about the product you want before committing to a long queue, because a store being open does not mean every filling remains available.

## Ask about storage at purchase
Fresh-fruit, yogurt and frozen products may need different handling. Follow the shop's instructions for the exact item you buy, including refrigeration, thawing and the use-by date. Do not apply a storage rule for one filling to the whole range.

## Our pick for a first visit
Try one fruit-led option and one contrasting filling if you are sharing. Keep the packaging details and flavour names so you can identify a favourite later. Branch listings, supermarket collaborations and prices in the original upload can change; check the current product label and retailer information.
