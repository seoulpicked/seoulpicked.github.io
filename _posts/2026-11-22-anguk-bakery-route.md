Anguk is a useful starting point for a bakery morning: three of the stops in our Short are clustered around one station. London Bagel Museum, Artist Bakery and Café Onion offer different reasons to visit, so decide what you want to eat before joining the longest line.

## London Bagel Museum: start with the bagel
Our video highlights the Anguk branch at **20 Bukchon-ro 4-gil**. Potato-cheese and truffle-butter bagels are two of the options featured; a filled sandwich is another choice if this is breakfast rather than a snack.

Treat the queue as part of your plan. The video describes CatchTable registration and different weekday and weekend arrangements. Check the branch's current listing before leaving: remote registration, on-site registration and the time you must return can change. An early arrival does not guarantee a particular product.

## Artist Bakery: make room for salt bread
At **45 Yulgok-ro**, Artist Bakery is the salt-bread stop in the route. The Short compares plain versions with richer fillings such as truffle butter, potato cheese and spring-onion cream. Choosing a simple bread at one shop and a filled option at another makes the comparison more interesting.

Check whether the waiting list is for takeaway or seating. Being nearby does not mean you can use one queue for both.

## Café Onion Anguk: finish with the setting
The final stop is **5 Gyedong-gil**, where the video pairs a hanok setting with pastries and coffee. Pandoro, salt bread and anbutter are among the items mentioned. Follow the café's current ordering and seating instructions rather than leaving bags on a table to hold it.

## A route that stays flexible
Begin at Anguk Station on Line 3. Check the busiest bakery's waiting arrangements first, then visit the other stops only if your return time allows. You do not have to eat at all three: one bakery purchase and one relaxed coffee stop can be a better morning than three long queues.
